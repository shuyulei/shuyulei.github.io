Shuyu Lei

PhD Candidate in Urban Planning

The University of Hong Kong

Faculty of Architecture · Hong Kong SAR, China

Age-friendly cities. Inclusive mobility.

I study what becomes visible when we stop assuming an average traveller. My PhD asks how ageing changes the time, effort and opportunities involved in everyday journeys, and what conventional measures of accessibility fail to see. Using mobility data and spatial analysis, I trace these hidden differences across transport systems and neighbourhoods. My broader work extends this lens to extreme heat and climate-responsive mobility.

01 / Research

Research & applied projects

First-author research

Age-friendly accessibility2026 · Hong Kong

How many minutes make an age-friendly city?View details

Most daily walking trips by older adults in Hong Kong fall within 5 to 12 minutes, depending on age and destination, rather than a single 15-minute benchmark. The framework combines age-specific thresholds with a hierarchy of everyday needs and a 3D pedestrian network.

Journal of Transport Geography

First-author research

Inclusive public transport2026 · Hong Kong

Where does a metro system fall short for older riders?View details

A smart-card framework measures travel-time gaps between older and younger riders, accounting for system familiarity and temporal variation, and identifies priority stations and routes for age-friendly improvements. Disparities are especially pronounced during weekday morning peaks.

Transportation Research Part A: Policy and Practice

Applied project · Team member

Applied GIS & urban accessibility2023 · Hong Kong

How can a 15-minute city become personal?View details

A participatory web tool that lets users set their walking pace, map a personalised 15-minute catchment, find essential amenities and comment on routes. Merit Award, Hong Kong CSDI Awards 2023.

Merit Award · Common Spatial Data Infrastructure (CSDI) Awards

Collaborative research · Second author

Heat and unequal mobility impacts2026 · New York City

Who has less flexibility to shift travel during extreme heat?View details

In New York, seniors and passengers with disabilities show the largest ridership declines, while Fair Fares riders show the least variation across the day, suggesting less flexibility to shift travel away from extreme heat.

Climate-responsive urban design2026 · Shanghai

Can the density that supports TOD also undermine thermal comfort?View details

Around Shanghai metro stations, higher population and building density are associated with greater summer thermal discomfort, while varied building heights and more vegetation are associated with better thermal comfort. GeoXAI points to a climate-responsive rethink of TOD’s design dimension.

Spatial networks & poverty2025 · Nairobi

What can street-network structure tell us about multidimensional poverty?View details

Comparing theory-driven logistic regression with XGBoost in Nairobi, the study identifies efficiency, severance, density and betweenness as key network predictors, with the theory-driven model performing better.

02 / About

Background & education

My work connects transport research, spatial analysis, and urban design.

My HKU departmental profile

Read my CV

I combine mobility data and geospatial analysis with surveys, interviews, and field observations to examine how accessibility varies across population groups and urban contexts. Much of my work focuses on older adults and other travellers whose mobility needs are not well captured by conventional planning approaches.

My research spans age-friendly public transport in Hong Kong, extreme heat and subway use in New York, and links between urban spatial networks and multidimensional poverty in Nairobi. Across these contexts, I examine how empirical evidence can support more inclusive and climate-responsive planning.

2020 / English

Strategic Planning for Hong Kong 2030+

HKU MSc Urban Planning · Strategic Planning Studio

Co-author · Student team

How can a long-term spatial plan guide decisions on the ground? Our HKU studio team reviewed Hong Kong's planning system and compared approaches in eight international cities.
